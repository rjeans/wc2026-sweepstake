The last four is set and there's a pause before the semi-finals, so here's a
good moment to look back. On opening day, the model had a firm view of how far
each team would go. Some teams politely ignored it.

### The overachievers

Top of the list, and comfortably: **Pete's Norway**. On 14 June the model gave
them a **7% chance of even reaching the quarter-finals**, a 0.1% shot at the
title - essentially off the board. They reached the quarters anyway, going out
only to Henry's England. And on the way they pulled off the single biggest
result of the tournament: **knocking Emily's Brazil out in the last 16**. A
rank outsider sending home a title pick.

Two more ran well past their billing. **Tega's Morocco** (a 38% quarter-final
chance) not only made the last eight but **put Pete's Netherlands out in the
round of 32** en route. And **Richard's Switzerland** turned a 20% quarter-final
chance into an actual quarter-final place. The four semi-finalists - France,
Spain, England, Argentina - were all among the pre-tournament favourites, so
they did roughly what was asked; the outsiders are the story.

### The underachievers

**Emily's Brazil** is the headline fall. Opening odds of around **22** to win
it, a 21% chance of reaching the semis - and out in the **last 16**, to Norway,
of all teams. The biggest name to go home early.

**Tega's Germany** went even earlier: out in the **round of 32** - the first
knockout round - beaten by Richard's Paraguay on penalties. A traditional power
gone almost before the knockouts began. **Sophie's Portugal** (a 21%
semi-final chance) fell in the last 16 to Ella's Spain, and **Pete's
Netherlands** (19%) went out in the round of 32 to Tega's Morocco.

### The owners' angle

**Pete's** tournament was a study in extremes: he owned the biggest
overachiever *and* one of the biggest flops - Norway soaring, Netherlands
sinking - his Norway even doing for Emily's Brazil along the way. He still ends
with no teams left and fourth, which tells you how fine the margins were.
**Tega** had the mirror image: Morocco ran deep while Germany went early, the
two roughly cancelling out at the foot of the table.

The quiet moral: these shocks shaped the standings as much as the favourites
did. Norway's run was most of what carried Pete into the top four; Brazil's
early exit stranded Emily; Germany's collapse hurt Tega. The teams that did
exactly what the odds asked are, fittingly, the four still standing.

## Odds watch

Into the lull, the market sits as a four-way between the semi-finalists' owners.
**Ella is favourite at ~3.09 (32.4%)** on Spain, with **Nichola ~3.72 (26.9%)**
and **Richard ~3.98 (25.1%)** close behind - Argentina reaching the last four
pulled Richard up to third and closing - and **Henry ~6.43 (15.6%)** completing
the set: **Spain 32%, France 27%, Argentina 25%, England 16%**. Everyone else is
off the board. The long-running divergence still holds - **Richard leads the
points table by thirty-four and the projected finish, yet is only third
favourite**, because the market prices the trophy and three teams are rated
near-level to lift it. The next move waits on the semi-final draw.
