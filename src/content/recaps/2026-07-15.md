Well, that's the heartbreaker. **Henry's England led at half-time and were an
hour from the final** - the Spain-England final the whole family wanted - and
then it slipped away. **Argentina came back to win 2-1**, and there's a cruel
irony in who did it: it was **Richard's Argentina**, the one team whose success
meant England's end. Heart said England; the table said Argentina. The
table won.

For **Henry** it's a cruel way to bow out of the title race. His England were
excellent all tournament - group winners, past Norway, a goal up in the
semi-final - and they'll feel this one. They drop into the third-place match,
where there's still a little to play for, but the final was right there.

For **Richard**, it means the lead is his again. Argentina reaching the final
banks **+40**, lifting him to **161 and back to the top**, twenty-two clear of
Ella. After a day in second, normal service resumes - just in time for the only
match that now matters.

Because here it is, the finish the whole thing has been building towards:
**Spain against Argentina. Ella against Richard. The World Cup final, and the
sweepstake, decided in one game.** Whoever's team lifts the trophy on the 19th
wins it all - if Argentina win, Richard takes it; if Spain win, Ella leaps to
207 and the crown is hers. Everything, on ninety minutes (or more).

First, though, a consolation tie with a small prize: the **third-place
play-off** on the 18th is **Nichola's France against Henry's England** - both
beaten semi-finalists, both chasing the **+20**. For Nichola it's the shot to
leapfrog Pete into fourth; for Henry, a chance to end a fine run on a win.
