The last eight is locked in, the simulator has churned through fifty thousand
tournaments, and it has opinions about where everyone finishes. Two players can
already put their feet up. One is quietly cruising. One is a coin-flip between
glory and the basement. Here's the projected table, top to bottom, with the
match that'll settle each fate.

**Richard — projected 1st (most likely 2nd).** The tortoise of the piece. He
leads on points, and thanks to the all-his-own **Argentina v Switzerland**
(12 Jul) he is *guaranteed* a semi-finalist - one of his teams knocks the other
out, and he wins either way. The lovely twist: the model still fancies him to
*finish* second more often than first, because he only tops the final table if
one of his teams actually lifts the cup. A commanding lead that, on its own,
isn't quite enough. Very on-brand.

**Ella — projected 2nd (the bookies' favourite).** The hare to Richard's
tortoise. Her **Spain** is the single most likely champion, which makes her the
shortest price in the market - but with less banked, she's feast-or-famine.
**Spain v Belgium** (10 Jul) is the springboard: clear it and she's flying.

**Henry — projected 3rd.** **England** snuck into the last eight and Henry with
them. His quarter-final is a straight family meeting: **Pete's Norway v Henry's
England** (11 Jul). Winner climbs, loser almost certainly slides.

**Pete — projected 4th.** He has *two* teams in the quarters - **Norway** and
**Belgium** - same as Richard, with one crucial difference: Richard's pair meet
each other, whereas Pete's sit on opposite sides of the bracket, so they don't
cancel each other out. Which sounds great until you notice both are outsiders,
and Belgium have to get past Ella's Spain first. Two bites at the cherry across
a busy weekend: **Belgium v Spain** (10 Jul) and **Norway v England** (11 Jul).

**Nichola — projected ~4th, but honestly anywhere from 1st to 8th.** The wild
card of the whole thing. She owns **France**, joint-favourite for the trophy,
yet has only banked 52 - so her range is comically wide. It all hinges on the
tournament's very first quarter-final, **France v Morocco** (9 Jul): win and
she's a genuine title contender, lose and she tumbles toward the bottom.

**Emily — 6th. Sophie — 7th. Both certain.** No teams left, nothing to play
for, and - delightfully - *nothing anyone can do about it*. The maths has
already sealed both placings: because **France and Morocco meet each other**
(9 Jul), exactly one of Nichola/Tega must leapfrog the pair, which pins Emily to
6th and Sophie to 7th in every single simulation. Put your feet up, you two.
You've earned the certainty.

**Tega — most likely 8th, but not done yet.** Marooned at the foot of the
projection, and yet: **Morocco are still alive**. Her escape hatch is that same
opening quarter-final - beat **Nichola's France** (9 Jul) and she vaults off the
bottom. One match, two players' fates: it decides whether Nichola soars or
Tega does.

### The four matches, in order

- **9 Jul — Nichola's France v Tega's Morocco.** The hinge. Sends one of them up
  and the other down, and quietly confirms Emily 6th / Sophie 7th either way.
- **10 Jul — Ella's Spain v Pete's Belgium.** The favourite's first real test;
  Pete's outside shot.
- **11 Jul — Pete's Norway v Henry's England.** A straight head-to-head between
  two mid-table rivals.
- **12 Jul — Richard's Argentina v Richard's Switzerland.** The one where the
  leader cannot lose. Guaranteed semi-finalist, whatever the score.

## Odds watch

The bracket being set has firmed things up front into a genuine three-way.
**Ella is favourite at ~3.36 (29.8%)** on Spain's title credentials, but
**Richard ~3.59 (27.8%)** and **Nichola ~4.19 (23.9%)** are right on her heels -
the title board reads **Spain 29.8%, Argentina 25.9%, France 23.9%**, barely six
points covering the three. **Henry (~8.30)** is the clear fourth; **Pete (~19.6)**
and **Tega (~72)** are the long shots, their teams alive but underdogs. And the
neat table-vs-market split endures: **Richard leads the points table and the
projected finish, yet sits second in the betting**, because the odds price who
lifts the cup while the projected finish rewards his high floor. Two ways of
being "top", one calm man sitting near the top of both. The mid-tier prices will
keep firming as the semi-final line-up resolves.
