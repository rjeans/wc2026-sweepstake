**Richard** remains untouched at the summit on **33**, but the race for the
places behind him turned over completely. **Henry** had the day of the
round - his **Senegal hammered Emily's Iraq 5-0** and his **Türkiye beat
Ella's USA 3-2** - hauling him out of the basement and right back into it.
**Pete** climbed too, his **Belgium thrashing Tega's New Zealand 5-1**,
while **Nichola's France put four past Pete's Norway (4-1)** and **Ella's
Spain edged Nichola's Uruguay 1-0**. The couples' meetings cut both ways.

It leaves everything below Richard desperately tight: **Emily 24**, **Pete
23**, then **Nichola and Ella level on 22**, **Henry 20**, and **Tega and
Sophie both on 18**. Six group matches still to play, but the shape of the
table is set: one runaway leader and seven within six points of each other.

With the groups all but done, the **Round of 32 has been drawn - and it is
brutal on the couples**. Three players land **all-their-own ties**:
**Henry's South Africa meets his own Canada**, **Emily's Brazil meets her
own Japan**, and **Nichola's France meets her own Sweden** - each guaranteed
a team in the last 16, but each forced to knock one of their own out. The
cross-ties carry real weight too: **Tega's Germany v Richard's Paraguay**,
**Pete's Netherlands v Tega's Morocco**, **Ella's USA v Nichola's Bosnia**,
**Sophie's Australia v Ella's Egypt**, and the pick of the lot - **Richard's
Argentina v Sophie's Cape Verde** - as the favourite begins his knockout
run. First ball, 28 June.

## Odds watch

multipliers now in view, the title is a **three-way photo finish at the
top**: **Richard ~5.1 (20%)** on Argentina, **Ella ~5.1 (20%)** on Spain,
and **Nichola ~5.5 (18%)** on France - all within a whisker. **Henry's
big day vaulted his price in to ~8.3 (12%)**, the day's biggest mover up,
with **Tega also bouncing back** after Belgium aside. The pack behind -
**Pete, Emily, Tega and Sophie** - sits clustered around **13**. The story
of the day is **Richard**: still nine points clear on the table, yet
**drifting in the title market (-7.6%)**, because his lead is built on cheap
group points while the prize money sits in the rounds ahead - and that
favours whoever still holds Spain, France and Argentina.
